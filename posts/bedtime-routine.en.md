Meditating before bed, or building a routine, is often suggested. This post looks at how much evidence there is behind it. The short version: mindfulness meditation seems to have improved sleep quality for some people, but the evidence is early, and meditation is not a treatment for sleep problems.

## One trial: older adults and six weeks

In a randomized study published in *JAMA Internal Medicine* in 2015, 49 people with an average age of 66 and moderate sleep problems were split into two groups. One group took mindfulness training for two hours a week for six weeks, the other had sleep hygiene education over the same time.

- The sleep quality score went from 10.2 to 7.4 on average in the mindfulness group and from 10.2 to 9.1 in the sleep hygiene group. The difference between the groups was 1.8 points.
- Insomnia symptoms, depression symptoms and fatigue also improved more in the mindfulness group than in the sleep hygiene group.
- The authors note that this is a short-term effect, measured right after the training.

The study is small, and the participants were in a particular age group and did not have severe insomnia. So it would not be right to generalize the result to everyone.

## The wider picture

A review published in 2019 pooled 18 randomized trials with 1,654 participants. The result has two parts:

- Compared with evidence-based sleep treatments, mindfulness meditation had no added effect on sleep quality. The strength of this evidence was low.
- Compared with non-specific control interventions, which only give people time and attention, it improved sleep quality with moderate evidence.

The authors call the findings preliminary: meditation may help with some aspects of sleep disturbance, but more research is needed.

## Is "sleep hygiene" enough?

Sleep hygiene advice, such as cutting back on caffeine, keeping regular hours and avoiding noise, usually sounds like it ought to work. A review published in 2015 says the individual recommendations are linked to sleep, but their direct effects have largely not been tested in the general population. In other words, how much each of these tips helps on its own is mostly unknown.

## A realistic evening routine

This is a suggestion, not a proven treatment.

1. Set aside a few minutes at the same time every evening, away from screens.
2. Try a short meditation or slow breathing. Five to ten minutes is enough.
3. If it does not work, do not blame yourself. Sleep problems have many causes.
4. If your sleep problems have lasted for weeks or affect how you function during the day, talk to a health professional.
